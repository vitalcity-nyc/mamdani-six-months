# Fact-check report — Mamdani's first six months: the promise ledger

**Run:** July 1–2, 2026, after the graphic was drafted. Four independent adversarial verification passes covered every row: (1) housing claims, (2) Albany/transit/tax/childcare claims, (3) public safety, education, wage, baby-basket and immigration claims, (4) the "Beyond the platform" items. Each pass attempted to refute claims, checked that cited links support the sentences they anchor and verified against primary sources (nyc.gov and Council releases, comptroller reports, the Council's Legistar API, governor's office releases) plus independent news coverage. A fifth pass sourced the "government that delivers" row (victory-speech and inaugural transcripts, Commission on Government Efficiency coverage, SPEED task force releases).

**Overall result:** the draft was substantially accurate. The audit found **one outright factual error**, **two claims that had been overtaken by events**, and a set of date, figure and attribution refinements — all fixed before publication, as logged below.

## Errors found and fixed

1. **Grocery stores (❌ → fixed).** Draft said reported costs "for the first two stores" grew to ~$70 million. Wrong: $70 million is the capital allocation for the whole five-store program (per city announcements); the first two stores account for a reported ~$40 million (about $30M East Harlem, $10M The Peninsula). Corrected and re-sourced to the mayor's office release and Bronx Times.
2. **Gang database (❌ staleness → fixed).** Draft said Mamdani "stayed publicly silent" on the NYPD gang database, mirroring a March 25 Gothamist article. In April he stepped back from his abolition pledge and signaled openness to a reformed database (Gothamist; THE CITY, April 28). Rewritten.
3. **Immigration legal services (❌ staleness → fixed).** Draft said budget funding was "not publicly confirmed." The FY27 budget adopted June 30 restores $86.4 million for immigration legal services (Council press release) — well short of the $165 million pledge. Rewritten with the Council citation.

## Refinements applied

- **Rent freeze vote date pinned to June 25, 2026** (Thursday-night vote; June 26 dates in some coverage are next-day reaction pieces). All row details verified: 7–1, six of nine Mamdani appointees, ~1M apartments, first-ever two-year freeze, Smyth resignation same day.
- **"Fast and free buses" advisor:** named in late April (April 27), not May; sentence reordered chronologically (bus lanes February → advisor April → Fair Fares June).
- **Corporate taxes:** added that the final state budget extended the existing top corporate rate rather than letting it lapse — "raised neither" stands.
- **CityFHEPS:** cost growth corrected to "$25 million to $1.1 billion by fiscal 2025" per the cited amNewYork source ($1.2B there is a comptroller projection); promise reworded to "drop the city's appeal in the lawsuit." The $175M/$125M/30,000 voucher figures and June 30 settlement verified against the Council release.
- **200,000 homes:** Block by Block release date pinned to May 26; Albany framing softened to "Hochul signaling opposition... and Albany providing no new capacity" (no formal rejection is on the record); $70B campaign borrowing figure verified (The Real Deal, Crain's); Hell Gate's "more ambitious than Bloomberg's or de Blasio's" verified verbatim; SPEED task force added (day-one executive order; May 13 reform package; ~8 months cut from affordable-project timelines, up to 2 years from rezonings).
- **Class size:** figures verified ($543M/~6,000 → $122M/~1,000; 77.5% cut supports "nearly 80%"); added that Albany granted the delay June 1 (full compliance pushed to 2029-30); savings re-attributed as ~$500M next year and, by one accounting (The City Reporter), nearly $3B across the financial plan.
- **Minimum wage:** bill verified live in Legistar (Int 0757-2026, Nurse, introduced March 10, in committee, not passed); small-employer endpoint corrected to $29 by 2031 plus inflation indexing (bill text) from the draft's "$30 by 2032" (a press inference).
- **Office of Community Safety:** March 19 date, office-not-department, ~$260M launch → $270M in the executive budget, B-HEARD centrality and Tisch estimate all verified; "911 calls" tightened to "calls for service" (her phrasing); Crain's added as the source for the dollars.
- **Potholes:** 100,000th pothole April 6 on Staten Island verified; the "most in first 100 days in over a decade" comparison attributed to the transportation department (it originates in the mayor's release; independent verification could not be confirmed).
- **Budget item:** $125.8B, 45–6 vote, June 30 adoption, $5.4B gap, flat 35,000 headcount, ~$8B one-shots and $7B+ FY28 gap (CBC $7.1B; comptroller $8.8B) all verified; vote citation moved to amNewYork because the Gothamist piece predates the vote; the balanced-budget-by-law framing confirmed (Financial Emergency Act / City Charter, GAAP).
- **Baby baskets:** promise contents, ~125,000 births, ~$19M and "not started" tracker status all verified (tracker fetched directly); added "as of its last update in April" because the tracker is stale; searches found no implementation steps to falsify "no visible action." (Note: the Adams-era "NYC Baby Boxes" hospital program is a different, pre-existing initiative.)
- **Wikipedia-only items re-sourced:** encampment sweeps (Jan. 5 pause, Feb. 18 reinstatement, DHS lead, one-week notice — amNewYork, Fortune, AP), deed-theft office (April 24 nyc.gov release, amNewYork; "concentrated in majority-Black and Hispanic neighborhoods" per lien data), gender-affirming care ($15M, June 26, provider fund + hotline + research + Queens adult pilot — nyc.gov, Gothamist).
- **State aid:** $8B over two years verified verbatim in The American Prospect; NBC New York added to support the "Albany refused" framing.
- **Childcare:** all figures verified — Jan. 8 announcement, ~2,000 seats, $1.7B statewide expansion, first-two-years-only state commitment; $73M confirmed as the enacted state funding ($75M in some coverage is the announced program cost — the graphic's "state funding" framing makes $73M correct).

## Items left as stated, with known limits

- **"No visible action" rows** rest on absence of coverage after multiple targeted searches — absence of evidence, stated as such in the graphic.
- **The MTA chair's ~$1 billion bus figure** dates to October 2025 and is a fare-revenue projection, not a formal cost study.
- **The comptroller's $340–380M pied-à-terre range** is his adjusted scenario beneath a $510M plausible ceiling; the graphic presents it as his lower estimate, which matches his bottom line.
- **The NYPD inspector general** point was removed from the SRG row rather than reworded: the post is filled by the Department of Investigation commissioner, not the mayor, and the vacancy predates the administration — keeping it risked implying a mayoral failure to appoint.
- Several nyc.gov pages block automated fetches (403); where that happened, claims rest on the release title/date plus two or more independent news accounts.

## Second verification pass (post-publication)

A second, fully independent three-agent audit re-checked every published claim against primary sources. Result: **no factual errors found** — all fifteen promise rows and five beyond-platform items verified, with the two load-bearing negatives (no fare-free bus pilot in the enacted state budget; no Strategic Response Group disbandment as of July 1) surviving targeted refutation attempts, and the previously-corrected grocery figure ($70M program-wide / $40M first two stores) confirmed correct. Two small refinements applied:

- **Grocery opening date** softened from "late 2027" to "2027" — sources state 2027 without the "late" specificity.
- **Budget one-shots** reworded from "roughly $8 billion in one-time resources" to name the mechanisms (reserves, pension-payment stretch, delayed costs), so it no longer collides with the separate ~$8 billion state-aid figure in the adjacent card. (The $8B one-shots figure is defensible per the Citizens Budget Commission's ~$7.8B count; the comptroller's narrower one-time figure is ~$6 billion.)

Both source URLs the second pass flagged as possibly dead (the victory-speech transcript and the gender-affirming-care Gothamist article) were confirmed to be the correct live URLs in the published page — the agent had tested guessed alternative slugs.

## Link integrity

Every cited URL in the graphic was checked for existence and support of its anchoring sentence. Two citation mismatches were fixed (the pre-vote Gothamist article on the 45–6 tally; PBS not containing the dollar figures). All links now support their claims.
